# DIY Trash Can Pad, Wall & Enclosure Plan

Nov 1, 2026 · @Brady

## Summary

You can build the same 10' x 7' raised can pad, wall and enclosure yourselves for roughly **$2,400–$5,200 in materials and rentals**, versus Quality YHM's **$8,562.80** quote. A painted cedar fence lands at the low end and Trex composite at the high end; trimming the pad depth saves another $400–$600.

Plan on **4–5 weekend days** for two people: one day of demo and digging, two days of wall and base, one day for pavers, and one or two for the fence and gate. Concrete for the fence posts needs a few days to cure before you hang panels.

Best time to build is **April–May 2027**. The quote is only valid 30 days, and paver base and poly sand don't like freezing weather, so there's no rush to beat it.

**Worth copying in the DIY build**

- **Fence posts don't bear on the wall.** Posts go in their own concrete footings behind the block, deeper than the wall base. A segmental block wall can't take the wind load of a fence on top of it.
- **Drainage behind the wall.** A perforated pipe and clean gravel behind the block keep water from pushing the wall out.
- **Matching pavers.** They suggest matching your back patio pavers, which is a good curb-appeal move.

**Gaps and things to watch**

- **No hose reel or spigot plan.** The render shows only two cans, so the pad may be tight once a reel is added (see Design & layout).
- **Block, cap, paver and sand colors are all TBD**, so the price could move.
- **Unmarked underground lines are your risk.** Downspout drains, irrigation, dog fence or private wiring damaged during the dig are an upcharge. The same risk applies DIY.
- **Pressure-treated pine fence.** It's the cheapest option and can't be painted for a few months after install while it dries.

## Design & layout

Keep the pad flush with the driveway, tight against the house wall, with the fence on the two open sides and a gate facing the driveway. That's the same footprint Quality YHM drew.

**Size it to what you store.** A standard 96-gallon cart is roughly 27–29" wide and 33–35" deep. Two carts plus a hose reel cart (about 24" wide) need about 8' of length with finger room, and about 4' of clear depth.

| Pad option | Footprint | Fits | Effect on the build |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| As quoted | \~10' along house x 7' out | 2 carts, reel, room for a 3rd cart or yard-waste bags | Tallest wall at the low corner (2.5–3'), most fill |
| Trimmed (recommended to price) | \~10' x 5' | 2 carts + reel, gate swings clear | Wall corner sits higher on the slope, so it's shorter; \~30% less block and fill |

Measure the slope before you decide: run a string level from the driveway edge out 5' and 7' and measure down to the grass. Every foot you pull the pad in on a falling lawn takes height off the wall.

**Gate and hose.** Put the gate on the driveway side, about 3–4' wide so a cart rolls straight through. Mount the hose reel at the end of the pad nearest the spigot and connect it with a 3–6' leader hose so the spigot stays where it is.

**Keep these clear:** dryer vent, foundation vents, downspout and its underground drain, and the siding itself. Leave a small gap between pavers and siding, and slope the pad about 1/8" per foot away from the house.

## Fence & gate options

For full color freedom, go with **paint-ready cedar** (cheapest) or **cellular PVC boards** (no rot, any color). For a near-exact house match, **clad the screen in your siding**. Composite looks great but locks you into its factory colors.

The enclosure is about 13–14' of 4'-tall fence plus one 3–4' gate, on 4–5 posts. Keep it at 4' or under: Pickerington caps front-yard fences at 4', and this spot is likely treated as front yard.

**Gate.** Build it in the same material on a steel gate-frame kit (such as Adjust-A-Gate) so it won't sag, or buy the matching kit for Trex or vinyl. Use heavy strap or self-closing hinges, a gravity latch, and a cane bolt to hold it open on trash day.

**Posts carry the fence, not the wall.** Set 4x4 ground-contact posts (or the system's posts) in concrete about 12" behind the wall face, roughly 36" deep so they're below the wall base and Ohio's frost line. Dig and set them before you stack the block, then build the wall and drainage stone around them.

Leave the spigot where it is and run a short leader hose to a reel inside the enclosure, on the end nearest the spigot. A wall-mount reel saves pad space; a lidded box hides the hose completely.

## Tools & rentals

**Rent (about $200–$300 total, approximate):**

- Plate compactor, 1–2 days (base and fill go down in 2–3" lifts, each compacted)
- Masonry cut-off saw or wet saw, 1 day, for paver and cap cuts

**Have on hand:** flat and round shovels, mattock or digging bar, wheelbarrow, hand tamper, post-hole digger, 4' level and torpedo level, string line with line level, stakes, tape measure, rubber mallet, rake and screed board (a straight 2x4 plus two 1" pipes), circular or miter saw, drill/driver, caulk gun, safety glasses, hearing protection and a dust mask for cutting.

## Permits, utilities & HOA

- [ ] **Call OUPS (811 or ohio811.org)** at least 2 working days before digging. It's free, but it only marks public utilities. Find your own downspout drain lines, irrigation, dog fence, landscape lighting and any gas line to a grill yourself before digging.
- [ ] **Pickerington fence permit.** The city generally requires a zoning permit for fences and limits front-yard fences to 4' ([city rule summary](https://cityrulelookup.com/blog/fences-rules-in-pickerington-oh)). Confirm with Pickerington Planning & Zoning and ask whether the 2.5–3' retaining wall needs anything; Ohio's residential code doesn't require a building permit for fences 6' and under ([Ohio overview](https://permitsguide.com/fence/ohio)).
- [ ] **HOA or deed restrictions.** If your subdivision has architectural review, submit the plan, fence material and color before buying anything.
- [ ] **Finished side out.** Pickerington expects the finished face of a fence toward the street or neighbor.

## Step-by-step build plan

Five working days for two people, spread over two or three weekends. Order block, pavers and bulk stone a week ahead so the pallets and stone pile are in the driveway on day 1.

1. **Prep (weeknight + OUPS):** Call 811, pick colors, get permit/HOA sign-off, mark private lines. Stake the pad and string it level off the driveway edge to confirm the wall height.
2. **Day 1 — Demo and dig:** Remove the 3 grasses, lilac and mulch (offer them to neighbors or replant in back). Strip the topsoil inside the footprint. Dig the wall trench 6" deeper than the block is tall plus the depth of one buried course, about 24" wide.
3. **Day 1–2 — Fence posts:** Dig post holes about 36" deep, 12" behind the wall line. Set posts plumb in concrete, brace them, and leave them a little tall to trim later. Let them cure while you build the wall.
4. **Day 2 — Wall base course:** Put 6" of compacted #304 in the trench, screed it dead level, and set the first course fully buried. This course decides how straight the whole wall is, so take your time.
5. **Day 2–3 — Stack, drain and backfill:** Lay the perforated pipe behind the first course, sloped to daylight at the low end. Stack courses with staggered joints; after every course, fill 12" behind the block with #57 stone and compact the fill behind that in 2–3" lifts with fabric between soil and stone. Step the wall down along the return as the grade rises toward the driveway.
6. **Day 3 — Caps:** Glue caps on with block adhesive, overhanging the face about 1".
7. **Day 4 — Pad:** Finish compacting the base to about 4" below driveway height, then screed 1" of bedding sand with a slight pitch away from the house. Lay pavers from the house corner out, cut the edges, install edge restraint, compact the pavers, then sweep in poly sand and mist it per the bag.
8. **Day 5 — Fence and gate:** Trim posts to height, add rails, hang pickets, boards or siding, then build and hang the gate with a 1/2–3/4" swing gap. Mount the hose reel and connect the leader hose.
9. **Finish:** Paint or stain once the wood is dry (cedar: a few weeks; pressure-treated: a few months). Top-dress and seed the disturbed lawn along the wall.
